Internal diameters are fixed by the dimensional standards NFPA 13 references (ASTM A135/A795, ASME B36.10M, ASTM F442, ASTM B88) and were verified against Wheatland, Bull Moose and Allied submittals — they do not vary by mill. Two values carry a caveat: 5" Sch 40 and copper Type L were confirmed from standard-derived tables rather than the paywalled standards themselves, and 3" CPVC is published as both 2.950 and 2.952 (a 0.14% volume difference).

2 Antifreeze solution

The annex needs solution density at the lowest expected temperature (DL) and at the highest (DH). Both come off the manufacturer's data sheet for the specific listed product and concentration.

3 Temperatures

Converted to absolute (Rankine) internally. Including the precharge air temperature is mandatory, not optional — see the citation in the edition bar above.

T1 and T2 are the chamber air temperatures when the piping sits at its lowest and highest expected temperature. They equal the piping temperatures only when the tank is in the same space as the piping — which is the normal case, and what the densities above are read at. If the chamber lives somewhere warmer, such as a heated riser room off an unheated attic, this method needs care beyond what this tool provides.

4 Pressures

Enter gauge pressures — 14.7 psi is added internally to get the absolute values the gas law needs. P0 and P1 are separate variables: precharge happens before installation, static is measured on the supply side of the backflow preventer.

Self-test

Scope. This sizes the expansion chamber only. It is not a complete antifreeze system design, and it does not replace the manufacturer's listing documents — where a listed product's data sheet gives its own sizing method or expansion table, that data sheet governs.

Method. NFPA 13 annex, one formula among several the annex acknowledges. Concepts are described in original wording with section numbers cited; no standard text is reproduced. Verify every value against the edition your AHJ enforces.
